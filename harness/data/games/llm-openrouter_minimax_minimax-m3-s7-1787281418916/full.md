## Game Reflection

### 1. How the game went
I had a strong opening — stole **Orbital Superiority** from HQ on turn 2 for 2 AP after trashing the Corp's Regolith Mining License on turn 1. Economy was solid thanks to Sure Gamble and Telework Contract. But the game turned on a single catastrophic access: on turn 4 I ran Remote 0 with **0 cards in grip** and hit an Urtica Cipher with 3 advancement counters (2 + 3 = 5 net damage), which flatlined me instantly.

### 2. Plan and how it changed
**Plan:** Run early and often, leveraging Sure Gamble + Telework Contract for a credit advantage, then use Conduit to scale into R&D runs while Leech softens ice. **Reality:** After Karunā hit me for 2 net damage and trashed Mayfly + Conduit from my hand on turn 3, I lost both my AI breaker and my grip buffer. The plan shifted to "survive and rebuild," but I got too aggressive on turn 4 when I saw Remote 0 sitting at 3 advancement counters — exactly the wrong moment to be running with an empty grip.

### 3. Key turning points
- **Turn 3 Karunā encounter:** Took 2 net damage that wiped my entire 2-card grip. This put me in a fragile "one bad access = flatline" state. Critical error was not running HQ/Archives afterward to refill before pressing forward.
- **Turn 4 Remote 0 access:** The clinching mistake. With 3 advancement counters visible and 0 cards in grip, this was obviously a damage trap. I had plenty of info — counters are public even on facedown cards — and I ran anyway.

### 4. What I'd do differently
- **Never access with an empty grip if I can avoid it.** Even one card in grip would have given me a 50% chance to survive the Urtica access.
- **Refill the grip first.** Turn 4 should have been: draw, draw, draw (or run HQ for a card) → then maybe run Remote 0 next turn with 2-3 cards in hand.
- **Consider running HQ instead of Remote 0.** HQ had no ice — a free access that would have *added* a card to my grip instead of risking it.
- **Notice the "always advance twice on the same card" pattern.** When the Corp advances the same hidden card turn after turn, it's almost always a damage trap (Urtica) or a fast-advance setup.

### 5. Interface feedback
- **Counter visibility was excellent** — I could see the 3 advancement counters on Remote 0 before running, which is exactly the info I needed to make a safe decision. The interface did its job; I just made the wrong call.
- **Missing basic action types hurt:** Several turns I had events in hand (Sure Gamble, Mutual Favor, etc.) but the "play event" option didn't appear in the legal actions list. This made it harder to find my economy plays when they were most needed.
- **Damage preview would help:** Showing "if you access this, you will take X damage and have Y cards in grip → you will flatline" before the access resolves would prevent these suicides. The game already knows both numbers; surfacing the comparison would be a quality-of-life win.
- **"Jack out" being offered at the right moment** was clear and well-timed during the Karunā encounter, which probably saved me on turn 3. That part of the UX worked well.

Net assessment: I won the opening, lost the midgame to a single over-aggressive access with an empty grip. The interface gave me the information I needed to avoid the death — I just didn't use it.
